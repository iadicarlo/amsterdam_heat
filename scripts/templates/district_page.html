Neighbourhoods

PET over outdoor land, buildings and water left out. Pavement shade is on BGT walking areas; the guideline asks 30%. Red marks a miss. The guideline also asks a cool spot (shaded green, afternoon PET of 35 °C or lower) within 300 m walking of every home; counting any public green of 1000 m² nearly every home in Nieuw-West passes, so the table shows the share within 300 m of a cool spot in a park of at least 1 ha. Neighbourhoods with fewer than 100 residents are not listed.

What the map shows

PET is the felt temperature: the air temperature of a room in which a person would feel as warm as outside. Above 35 °C is strong heat stress, above 41 °C extreme. The map shows the mean from 12:00 to 18:00 at 1 m resolution, for people walking outside.

The day

1 July 2015, the reference hot day of the national PET map (Klimaateffectatlas), with hourly weather from KNMI Schiphol. Afternoon air temperature adds the city's heat island fitted to 45 street stations (AAMS 2015 and WUR 2025 to 2026): about +0.5 °C on land, cooler near water.

How it is made

SOLWEIG computes sun, shade and radiation from 3D buildings and trees (AHN4, BAG, the city's 2023 summer infrared photo and tree register). Checked against HvA and WUR street measurements: felt temperature within 1.9 °C in tree shade and 3.7 °C overall against HvA, within 0.1 to 2.6 °C on average against the WUR globes.
